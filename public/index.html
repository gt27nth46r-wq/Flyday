Thresholds & runway

Runway heading is used only in your browser, to estimate crosswind and check wind alignment from the forecast wind - it's never sent to the server. Note: forecast wind direction is true north and runway numbers are magnetic, so estimates can be off by however much local magnetic variation is at your airport.

How a day (and each hour) gets rated

  • Good — high pressure over the area, and forecast wind under your threshold
  • Marginal — only one of those two holds
  • Bad — neither holds, or a low is moving in (sharp 3-hour pressure fall), or the isobars are packed tight nearby, or - if you've entered a runway - the wind is more than 15° off that runway's line
  • Unknown — the model didn't return data that far out yet

Flyday is a planning aid built on the NWS 7-day gridpoint forecast (pressure, wind, ceiling, visibility, and a nearby gridpoint used to estimate pressure gradient) and aviationweather.gov METAR/TAF (current conditions, near-term flight category). The gradient reading is a proxy, not a hand-analyzed surface chart. It is not a substitute for an official preflight weather briefing — always check current METARs, TAFs, AIRMETs/SIGMETs and NOTAMs before you fly.
