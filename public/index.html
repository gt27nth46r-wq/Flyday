How a day (and each hour) gets rated

Flyday scores each hour and day against a checklist modeled on a standard GA "good flying weather" reference — surface wind, gust spread, crosswind, visibility, ceiling, dew point spread, pressure pattern, precipitation, TAF trend, and convection. Each item is Green, Yellow, or Red. The overall rating isn't just "worst item wins" - a single stray Red on a soft signal like wind or gust spread, surrounded by otherwise-Green items, reads as Marginal rather than Bad. But a handful of items (ceiling, visibility, pressure pattern, precipitation, TAF-flagged hazards, icing, convection, and crosswind) are treated as critical: a Red on any one of those is a real go/no-go stop that good wind and pressure elsewhere can't outweigh. Multiple independent problems, even non-critical ones, still add up to Bad.

  • Good — every checked item is Green
  • Marginal — a caution flag or two, but nothing serious enough to call it Bad
  • Bad — either a critical item is Red (ceiling, visibility, pressure pattern, precipitation, a TAF-flagged hazard, icing, convection, or crosswind beyond every runway), or multiple independent problems are stacking up together
  • Unknown — no checked items had data yet (rare, usually day 7)

Turbulence, icing, and convection are checked against real current AIRMET/SIGMET/convective SIGMET data for the airport's location, not guessed - but only for roughly the next 24 hours, since those products are short-fuse and can't meaningfully forecast day 5 of a 7-day outlook. Beyond that window (or if the check fails), those rows fall back to a coarser proxy or show N/A. A new "est. convective cloud base" row uses the classic adiabatic-lapse-rate estimate from temperature and dew point to flag when cumulus bases are likely to form low, even on an otherwise clear-looking forecast. Expand "Full checklist" on any day for the item-by-item breakdown. A few rows — fronts (actual position), cloud type/vertical development, PIREP ride reports, and winds aloft — are still shown as N/A: there's no free real-time data source Flyday can reach for those, so it says so rather than guessing. Check those separately in a full preflight briefing.

Flyday is a planning aid built on the NWS 7-day gridpoint forecast (pressure, wind, ceiling, visibility, temperature, dew point, ice accumulation, and a nearby gridpoint used to estimate pressure gradient) and aviationweather.gov METAR/TAF (current conditions, near-term flight category). The gradient reading is a proxy, not a hand-analyzed surface chart. It is not a substitute for an official preflight weather briefing — always check current METARs, TAFs, AIRMETs/SIGMETs, PIREPs, and NOTAMs before you fly.
